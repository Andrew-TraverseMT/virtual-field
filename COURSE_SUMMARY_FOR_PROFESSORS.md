# Virtual Field Geology Basecamp: Course Summary for Faculty Review

## Course Snapshot

This course is a 3-week virtual geology field camp built around digital mapping, structural interpretation, and applied geohazard analysis.

- Format: Fully online or hybrid-compatible
- Length: 3 weeks
- Assignments: 12 graded assignments
- Estimated student workload: about 49.5 hours total
- Total points: 600
- Core platforms:
  - Visible Geology (interactive 2D/3D geology and stereonet work)
  - Leeds Virtual Landscapes (virtual field mapping environments)

## Who This Course Is For

This design is a good fit for:

- Introductory to intermediate structural geology and mapping instruction
- Programs needing virtual field alternatives or supplements
- Instructors who want authentic map-plus-cross-section workflows without travel logistics

Students practice professional outputs throughout the course:

- Geological maps with standard cartographic elements
- Cross sections with topographic profiles
- Structural interpretations (including stereonets where appropriate)
- Applied hazard or human-impact reasoning tied to mapped evidence

## Learning Design

The course progresses in three stages:

1. Foundations in map-pattern reasoning (contours, dip/strike, Rule of V's)
2. Full virtual landscape mapping (from introductory to advanced structural complexity)
3. Independent capstone project with proposal and final submission

A consistent pattern appears across assignments:

- Observation and model interaction
- Visual evidence submission (screenshots, annotated maps, diagrams)
- Written interpretation using geologic reasoning
- Applied relevance (hazard, infrastructure, or land-use implications)

## Weekly Arc and Workload

### Week 1 (Foundations and Introductory Mapping)

- Hours: 15.0
- Points: 185
- Focus:
  - Topographic interpretation in 2D/3D
  - Dip/strike effects on outcrop pattern
  - Rule of V's mastery
  - Intro/intermediate map and cross-section production in virtual landscapes

### Week 2 (Advanced Structural and Basin Analysis)

- Hours: 18.5
- Points: 235
- Focus:
  - Stereonet interpretation and fold geometry
  - Complex real-world mapping (Rhoscolyn)
  - Basin-scale stratigraphic reasoning and drill-core correlation
  - Reservoir/seal logic and CO2 storage viability analysis

### Week 3 (Synthesis and Independent Work)

- Hours: 16.0
- Points: 180
- Focus:
  - Student-built 3D model integrating map and section logic
  - Capstone proposal and execution
  - Final integrated deliverable set with hazard or human-impact assessment

## Assessment Model

Assessment is rubric-driven and artifact-rich. Most assignments grade a combination of:

- Technical accuracy (map boundaries, symbols, structural consistency)
- Subsurface reasoning quality (cross sections and geometry)
- Structural interpretation (including stereonet products when required)
- Application quality (hazard/impact assessment with specific map evidence)
- Communication and presentation standards

The capstone final project is the largest single assessment (100 points) and requires integrated geological reasoning across all major skill areas.

## Capstone Flexibility

The final project supports multiple pathways:

- Remote structural geology
- Remote geomorphic or Quaternary mapping
- Remote lithologic mapping
- Field structural geology (optional, on public land)
- Field geomorphic or engineering geology

All pathways require at least:

- Geological map
- Geological cross section
- Human-impact or geohazard assessment
- Stereonet, when the selected project type is structural

## Adoption Considerations for Another Professor

### Strengths

- Strong scaffold from fundamentals to independent synthesis
- Authentic outputs aligned with field-geology practice
- Built-in hazard/impact framing improves real-world relevance
- Flexible capstone supports different student interests and contexts

### Requirements

- Instructor familiarity with basic structural geology mapping conventions
- Access to Visible Geology and Leeds Virtual Landscape resources
- Student support for screenshot/PDF submission workflow
- Time for rubric-based review of maps, sections, and written interpretation

### Typical Implementation Pattern

A professor can adopt this as:

- A compact 3-week intensive
- A module sequence embedded within a longer structural geology course
- A virtual alternative when field access is constrained

## Bottom Line

This is a high-structure, evidence-driven virtual field course that emphasizes practical mapping competence, structural interpretation, and applied geologic decision-making. It is especially attractive for instructors who want rigorous field-style outcomes in an online or hybrid format without sacrificing authentic geoscience workflows.
